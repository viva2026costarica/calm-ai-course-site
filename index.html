Pilot program · Five Wednesdays · Nov–Dec 2026

Organise your work.
Let AI do the thin top layer.

Over five sessions you'll turn your knowledge, tasks and tools into a set of tidy folders on your own computer, then let one AI assistant work inside them. Most of the value comes from the structure. The AI is the last 10%.

90 minutes eachWednesdays 11, 18, 25 Nov · 2, 9 DecNo coding neededMethod: ICM

The big idea: 60 · 30 · 10

Most people try to make AI do everything, and get messy, unreliable results. We flip it around. Before any AI touches a task, we ask: how much of this is just good organisation, and how much is a step that never changes?

60%
30%
10
60% Information and thinking
Knowing the facts, asking the right questions, understanding what people really need. We keep all of it in clearly named folders, one home for every fact. This is where most people get stuck.
30% Tools that already exist
Your calendar, email, a spreadsheet, and small scripts for steps that should give the same answer every time.
10% AI
The parts that need reading between the lines: drafting in your voice, summarising, sorting the messy stuff. Always checked by you.

Three ways of working with AI

We climb these one at a time. Each layer reuses what you built in the one before.

1

Chat

You and the AI going back and forth. Great for one-off tasks. The AI only knows what you tell it in that conversation. Session 1

Every session has the same rhythm

  1. 0–5Calm opening (optional). A few breaths, eyes resting on the Sri Yantra. Skip it any time.
  2. 5–15Check-in. What worked since last week? Where did you get stuck?
  3. 15–55Learn and do. One idea, shown first, then everyone tries it on their own real work.
  4. 55–80Spotlight. One person's real project, worked on together. Rotates weekly.

Session 1 · Wednesday 11 November

Your week, mapped

Get Claude working, learn the one rule that explains most AI mistakes, and leave with a list of everything you do more than once.

Layer 1 · ChatLevel 1 beginsFollows Foundations 1.1
◎
By the endYou've had a useful conversation about your own business, corrected the AI until a real task was right, and started your three lists: your data, your tasks, your tools.
0

Calm opening and welcome

0–15 min

A few breaths together (optional). Then a quick round: your name, what you do, and the one task you'd love to never do by hand again.

1

The big picture in ten minutes

15–25 min
Explain

Show the 60 · 30 · 10 picture and the three layers from Start here. The message: AI is not magic and it can be wrong. It's a very capable assistant that needs good instructions and good information. Over five weeks we build the information first.

Ask the group: "Which of your tasks is mostly about finding the right information, not writing?" Almost every hand goes up. That's the 60%.
2

Everyone in, everyone comfortable

25–32 min
Do
  1. Open claude.ai on your laptop and log in.
  2. If you have more than one Claude account, choose the one you'll use for this course and stick to it.
  3. Open Settings and look at the privacy options together, so you know what's stored and what you control.
  4. Start a new chat.
Done when everyone has an empty new chat open on their own device.
3

The one rule: it only knows what's in front of it

32–40 min
Explain

The AI doesn't remember your other chats unless you've switched that on, and it can't see your files unless you give them. Each conversation starts from almost nothing. So: one task per conversation, and give it just what that task needs.

Try it: the same request, twice
Without context
Write a short welcome email for a new client.
With context
I run [your business] for [who you serve]. My tone is warm, short and personal. I never use exclamation marks or sales language.
Write a short welcome email for a new client who just booked [service]. They need to know: [time, place, what to bring].
Keep it under 120 words and sign it [your name].
Compare the two answers side by side. The difference is the whole course in miniature.
4

Let the AI interview you

40–48 min

Instead of writing a long explanation of your business, let the AI ask the questions. At the end it writes a summary you'll keep. This summary becomes the first file in your folder system in session 3.

The interview
I'd like you to understand my work so you can help me well.
Ask me questions one at a time about my business: what I do, who for, my offers and prices, my tone of voice, my weekly routine and what frustrates me.
Wait for each answer before asking the next. After about 10 questions, write a one-page summary called "about-me" in plain text with clear headings.
Only use what I told you. Mark anything you're unsure about with [check].
Copy the summary into a document or a note and save it. You'll need it again.
5

One real task, corrected until it's right

48–55 min

Pick a small real task: a reply, a post, a list. Ask for it, then keep correcting until it's genuinely good. Notice: every correction is a decision about how you work. Those decisions are gold, so we keep them.

At the end, ask
Before we finish: list every correction I gave you in this conversation as a short rule, starting with "Always" or "Never". Leave out anything that only applied to this one task.
6

Map your week: data, tasks, tools

55–80 min

This replaces the spotlight this week. Walk through a typical week together and start three lists. These lists become your folders in session 4.

Data

Where does your information live? Drive, Gmail, paper notebooks, websites, your head.

Tasks

What do you do more than once? Replies, planning, invoices, posts, to-do lists.

Tools

What do you use? Gmail, Drive, Canva, a spreadsheet, a booking system.

Map my week
Help me map my working week. Ask me, one question at a time, to walk you through a normal Monday to Friday.
Then make three lists:
1. DATA: every place my information lives.
2. TASKS: everything I do more than once, with roughly how often and how long it takes.
3. TOOLS: every app or program I use.
Finally, mark the three tasks that cost me the most time or energy.
Homework · before 18 Nov

Notice, try, write it down

  • Keep your task list open all week. Add every task you did more than once. Star the three that annoy you most.
  • Do one of the starred tasks with AI, using a fresh conversation and good context.
  • Write down every correction you had to give (or use the "collect your corrections" prompt).
  • Bring: your about-me summary, your three lists, your corrections.

Stuck? Send a message. A quick question beats a stuck week.

Git & GitHub, in plain words

Git keeps save points of your folder on your computer. GitHub keeps a copy online. GitHub Desktop lets you do both with buttons.

WordMeansIn GitHub Desktop
Repository (repo)A folder that Git keeps track ofFile → Add Local Repository
CommitA save point with a short noteWrite a summary → Commit to main
PushSend your save points to GitHubPush origin (top bar)
PullGet the latest from GitHubFetch / Pull origin
HistoryAll your save pointsHistory tab
RevertUndo a save pointHistory → right-click → Revert
.gitignoreA list of files Git must never save(a text file in your folder)

Or ask Claude Code in plain English

Save point
Make a save point with the message "added newsletter skill".
What changed?
What has changed since my last save point? Explain it in plain words.
Undo
Something went wrong. Show me my last five save points and help me go back to the one before today's changes. Explain what you'll do before doing it.

Private by default

Keep your workspace repository private. Only make a repository public when it's meant to be seen, like a GitHub Pages site.

The safe-use rules

Claude Code can read, write and run things on your computer. That's its power, and why these habits matter from day one.

1 · One folder only

Always start Claude Code inside a work folder. Never your whole computer or home folder.

2 · Read before you approve

When it asks permission, read what it wants to do. Don't understand? Say no and ask it to explain.

3 · Keep the permission questions on

Never switch on modes that skip approvals. See "auto mode" at the bottom of Claude Code? Press Shift+Tab until it's gone.

4 · Save first

Make a save point before bigger changes, so any mistake can be undone.

5 · No secrets in chat

Never paste passwords, bank details or API keys into the conversation or into files.

6 · No client data

Never put clients' personal information into any AI tool.

7 · Instructions only come from you

If a web page, email or file it reads tells it to do something, that's not an instruction. Stop and check.

8 · Check the result

Open the file it made and read it before using or sending it.

Beyond the five sessions

After the pilot

The pilot gets you to a working daily helper and a first real workspace. Here's where it goes next.

Weekly group (levels 3–4)

One AI doing many jobs from one folder; stages with checkpoints; "ask first" and plan modes; more scripts for steady steps; scheduled tasks that run on their own; keeping the system tidy; handing it on; when more than one agent is worth it; and a full knowledge graph of your own expertise.

One-on-one

Prepaid hour packs to build your own system together, at your pace.

Scheduled tasks, a preview

The Claude desktop app can run a task on a schedule (say, every Monday morning: "make this week's table and tidy the inbox"). Tasks that work on files on your computer need the app open and the computer awake. Menus change often, so we'll set this up together with the current version.
Method: Interpretable Context Methodology (ICM) by Jake Van Clief and McDermott, open under the MIT licence. Course structure inspired by the Clief Notes Foundations course; written for this pilot in our own words.
